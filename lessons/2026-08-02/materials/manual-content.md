## Overview

The Jewish people returned from 70 years of Babylonian captivity to rebuild Jerusalem and the temple. Three leaders accomplished this "great work": Zerubbabel oversaw temple reconstruction, Ezra restored commitment to God's law, and Nehemiah led the rebuilding of protective walls. Their experiences parallel our own spiritual work today.

## Section Headings & Content (full manual)

### The Lord inspires people to accomplish His purposes (Ezra 1)
King Cyrus of Persia was divinely inspired to enable Jewish exiles to return and rebuild the temple.
- How do you see the Lord working through people around you, including non-Church members?
- What does this reveal about the Lord and His work?
- Related: Isaiah 44:24–28

### The house of the Lord is a place of joy (Ezra 3:8–13; 6:16–22)
After the Babylonians destroyed the first temple, the returning Jews celebrated its reconstruction with great emotion.
- How might witnessing the temple's destruction have affected you personally?
- What emotions did the people experience during rebuilding (see Ezra 3:8–13; 6:16–22)?
- Why is temple construction a cause for celebration?
- Related: Psalm 137; 2 Kings 25:1–10; 2 Chronicles 36:17–19

### God has an important work for me to do (Ezra 4–7; Nehemiah 2; 4; 6)
Opposition constantly threatened the rebuilding efforts, yet leaders persisted through faith and determination.
- What does "a mind to work" in the Savior's service mean to you? (Nehemiah 4:6)
- What signifies having "the good hand of … God upon [you]" during service? (Nehemiah 2:8; see also 2:18; Ezra 7:6, 9, 27–28)
- Recommended: President Dieter F. Uchtdorf, "We Are Doing a Great Work and Cannot Come Down" (Ensign/Liahona, May 2009, 59–62)

### I am blessed when I study the scriptures (Nehemiah 8) — LESSON FOCUS
After generations without direct access to "the book of the law of Moses," Ezra publicly read scripture to assembled Jews.
- What details in Nehemiah 8:1–12 reveal people's reverence for God's word?
- How do your actions demonstrate your personal feelings about scripture?
- Related resource: *Teachings of Presidents of the Church: Ezra Taft Benson* (2014), pages 115–124 (Chapter 8, "The Power of the Word")

## Focus passage: Nehemiah 8:1–12

- **8:1–8** — All the people gather as one and ask Ezra to bring the book of the law of Moses. He reads it publicly from morning until midday to everyone old enough to understand, and "the ears of all the people were attentive." They stand as it's opened, answer "Amen, Amen" with hands raised, then bow and worship. Levites help the people understand ("gave the sense, and caused them to understand the reading").
- **8:9–12** — The people weep upon hearing the law. Nehemiah, Ezra, and the Levites tell them: "This day is holy unto the Lord your God; mourn not, nor weep... Go your way, eat the fat, and drink the sweet... for the joy of the Lord is your strength." The people go away to celebrate "because they had understood the words that were declared unto them."
